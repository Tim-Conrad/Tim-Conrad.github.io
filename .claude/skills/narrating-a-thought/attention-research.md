# Attention research for narrated thoughts

What research says about holding a listener's and a reader's attention, and what each finding means for a narrated thought. Checked on 12 Nov 2026. Where only a summary or abstract was read, the entry says so.

## Findings

| Finding | Evidence | What it means here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People learn more from a picture with spoken words than from a picture with printed words (modality principle). | Fiorella and Mayer 2022, review of 76 studies, median d = 0.65 [1]. | Let the voice explain the figure. Do not put the same explanation as text next to the figure. |
| Printed text that repeats the narration word for word, next to a picture, hurts learning (redundancy principle). It does not hurt when the text is very short. | Mayer's reviews, d about 0.7 to 0.87 [2][3]. | Spoken parts stay folded until the voice reaches them. Under a figure, show only the paragraph that the voice reads now, not the whole description. |
| Cues that show what matters help (signaling). | Mayer's reviews, d about 0.46 to 0.69 [2][3]. | The four colours and the phrase marker are cues. A cue must point at what the voice says now, and at nothing else. |
| Learner-paced segments beat one continuous stream (segmenting). | Mayer's reviews, d = 0.70 [2]. | Chapters, skip 15 s, tap a paragraph to play from there. |
| Interesting but irrelevant extras lower learning, more so in short lessons (seductive details). | Rey 2012. Sundararajan and Adesope 2020, 68 experiments. A 2026 multi-level meta-analysis, g = -0.16 [4]. Summaries only. | Bridges carry the argument forward. No jokes, anecdotes or sound effects that do not serve the point. |
| Synchronized word highlighting captures the eyes, but it did not improve children's learning over unsynchronized reading while listening. Weaker readers liked it most. | Gerbier, Bailly and Bosse 2018 [5]. Abstract and summaries only. | A strong word-by-word marker pulls the eye and does not teach more. Use a calm phrase marker. This matches what Tim felt with the solid word block. |
| Reading while listening helps weaker readers and doubled reading volume in one study. Results on comprehension are mixed. | Review of reading-while-listening studies [6]. | The read-along mode is a reason to read, not proof of better understanding. Keep audio-only listening a full experience too. |
| Listening alone produced more mind wandering, weaker memory and less interest than reading aloud or silent reading. | Varao Sousa, Carriere and Smilek 2013 [7]. In everyday settings, outside distraction mattered more than mind wandering (2018) [8]. | Pure listening needs extra help: signposts, short recaps, an outro. Readers on a phone get distracted, so resume and "Back to the voice" matter. |
| Short tests during a lecture halved mind wandering (40% without them) and improved retention. | Szpunar, Khan and Schacter 2013, PNAS, 21-minute lecture [9]. | Open idea: a short question at a section end ("Pause here: which file reads you?"). Not tried yet. |
| Shorter videos held viewers longer. | Guo, Kim and Rubin 2014, 6.9 million sessions on edX [10]. The often quoted "6 minutes" comes from summaries, not checked in the paper. | Keep chapters short, about 1 to 3 minutes. Show the length of each chapter. |
| Narration is usually 150 to 160 words per minute. Listeners picked about 165 wpm as comfortable. Comprehension holds up to about 1.25x and drops near 1.5x to 2x. | Production norm and summaries of speech-rate studies [11]. One ad study found about 180 wpm best [11]. | Default 1x, offer 0.9x, 1.15x and 1.3x. Do not offer 2x. |
| A modern synthetic voice taught as well as a human voice. The human voice still felt more human and engaging. | Craig and Schroeder 2017; Mayer and DaPra 2012 [12]. Summaries only. | ElevenLabs is fine for learning. Choose a calm voice with a natural pace. Keep the same voice for all thoughts, so it becomes familiar. |
| Writing for the ear: short sentences of varied length, one idea per sentence, signposts and previews, deliberate repetition of the main point. | Radio and NPR training material [13]. Practice guidance, not experiments. | Bridges preview the next question. Repeat the key term when a section returns to it. |
| Describe a diagram by its point, not by every detail. Long narrated descriptions of flow charts are hard to follow. Present processes as ordered steps. | WGBH/NCAM guidelines for STEM images; OCALI audio description tips [14]. | Figure walk-through: kind of drawing first, then axes or structure, then each part in a fixed order, then the takeaway. |
